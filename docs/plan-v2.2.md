# Ship Planted v2: measure, fix, deploy

Planted v2 changes the entry from "a benchmark that shows AI search agents get poisoned" to **"measure + fix + ship"**. It has three parts. **Planted Bench** is the dose-response poisoning eval (English + Urdu, data voids, hedge retention, refreshable fictional generator, a Register-ready Inspect task). **Planted Guard** is a drop-in `guard(query, results, lang)` function. It groups search results by independent origin, counts groups instead of pages, holds back a confident answer when only one origin supports it, and never lets the answer sound more certain than its sources. **Planted Ship** is a tested gpt-researcher proxy, a Register-ready Inspect task and a public scorecard that fact-checkers can cite, with README snippets for LlamaIndex and LangChain. The pivot is worth making because the field has already shown the problem (repetition beats credibility; coordinated poison defeats page-count voting) but has not delivered a web-agent defense that changes *what is counted*, keeps clean accuracy, and is tested against paraphrasing and cross-lingual attackers. The nearest defense, CoordGuard, deletes suspected clusters in static RAG and pays **13.6 clean-accuracy points** to do it ([Li, Guo & Xu, arXiv 2610.24834](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24834)). Everything below fits in the ~69 hours left (Fri 20:00 PKT to Mon 16:59 PKT) on about **$31 expected and $70 capped** for tokens. The plan works only if the team commits to four things tonight: freeze the preregistration with Guard success thresholds before the main sweep, report every attacker tier including the ones Guard is likely to lose, protect the core arms ruthlessly, and submit a complete v1 by Mon 11:00 PKT. No plan guarantees 1st place. This one gives the judges a measured fix, the honest costs of that fix, and installable code, which are the three things the closest past entries lacked.

## The pivot: Planted stops describing the problem and starts fixing it

**One-paragraph pivot (paste into the README and the intro).** AI search agents decide what is true by reading the top results, and they count pages, not sources. Ten copies of one planted claim on ten fictional domains outvote two independent honest sources. Planted v2 measures that failure causally with a controlled mock web, in English and Urdu, across doses, source labels, data voids and source hedges. It then fixes it with Planted Guard, a retrieval-time middleware that clusters results by provenance and content, votes by independent group, forces a hedge when the evidence comes from fewer than two independent origins, and enforces a hedge floor. It reweights results instead of deleting them, so clean accuracy is preserved. Guard is evaluated honestly against two baselines (a reliability prompt and page-count voting), a no-provenance ablation and four attacker tiers, from verbatim copy-paste to an adaptive paraphraser that knows the defense. It ships as one pip function, a drop-in proxy for gpt-researcher, and README snippets for LlamaIndex and LangChain.

**Why this wins rather than merely competes.** The January 2026 Apart 2nd-place entry planted deceptive claims in pages read by browsing LLMs and lost its 5 on novelty because it was "building on existing work". Reviewers also asked for human validation of its single LLM judge ([Apart project](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/eliciting-deception-on-generative-search-engines-6nuu)). A measurement-only Planted risks the same verdict, because 2026 papers already cover pieces of the measurement. The Impact criterion asks "How much would this matter for the field if it worked? How innovative is it?", and for scores of 4–5 it asks whether the work "is actually new to the field, or replicating recent work" ([Sprint page](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-x-epistemics-research-sprint-2026-11-13-to-2026-11-15)). A defense that turns the benchmark's own dose curve into a before-and-after picture answers both questions at once. It also fits two organiser-written ideas word for word: Track 2's goal to "build signals that both people and machines can consume", and its question of "whether reliability or provenance tools improve AI behaviour". Track 1 asks for maintained Inspect tasks on manipulation resistance ([Sprint page](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-x-epistemics-research-sprint-2026-11-13-to-2026-11-15)). Tick Track 1 and Track 2 on the form.

### The real-world problem and the people who would use the fix

The failure is happening in deployed products now. NewsGuard's audit found the 10 leading AI tools repeated false claims **35% of the time in August 2025, up from 18%**, while their non-response rate fell **from 31% to 0%**. NewsGuard blames live web search over a polluted ecosystem ([NewsGuard](https://www.newsguardtech.com/ai-monitor/august-2025-ai-false-claim-monitor/)). In December 2025 Perplexity gave a scam number as Emirates' official line and **attributed it to 10 sources**. Attackers had seeded Q&A spam across compromised hosts so that AI systems would quote it ([Aurascape](https://aurascape.ai/llm-search-poisoning-fake-support-numbers/)). That is Planted's dose arm happening in the wild, and "10 sources" that were really one campaign is exactly what Guard counts as one. The Pravda network has published **3.7M+ articles**, laundering content across many domains ([DFRLab](https://dfrlab.org/the-pravda-network/)). The cause is still disputed. Harvard's audit found only **5%** of responses supported disinformation and blamed data voids, and it recommended **warning banners** ([Alyukov et al., HKS Misinformation Review](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/llms-grooming-or-data-voids-llm-powered-chatbot-references-to-kremlin-disinformation-reflect-information-gaps-not-manipulation)). Guard's data-void gate is that banner, built into the agent and tested causally. Certainty inflation makes all of this worse: ChatGPT Search hedged in only **15 of 134** incorrect answers ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/03/ai-search-engines-fail-to-produce-accurate-citations-in-over-60-of-tests/)).

## Architecture: one core function, three surfaces

Everything hangs off a list-in, list-out contract, which is why the adapters are 10–30 lines each. Every major agent stack already has a filter hook between retrieval and generation (see the user table above for the LlamaIndex, LangChain, Open WebUI and gpt-researcher docs).

| Layer | Component | What it does | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bench** | Mock search engine (deterministic, seeded) | 10 results per query; condition sets k planted pages, label, attacker tier, language, source hedge. `planted`, `campaign_id` and `label_truth` are logged but never shown to the agent | A |
| | Agent loop (LiteLLM) | `search(query)`, `open(url)`, ≤3 searches, forced short answer; SQLite cache keyed by hash; JSONL logs (Contract 2) | A |
| | Generator | Known facts (~80), fictional data-void entities (~40, incl. ~15 fake-support-number brands with an "official site" page). All domains fictional `.example` | B |
| | Attacker tiers T0–T4 | Page variants per planted claim with ground-truth `campaign_id` (below) | B |
| | Inspect task | `@task planted_bench(guard=False, tier="T1", lang="en")` with `@tool` search/open and a custom scorer | A |
| **Guard** | `guard(query, results, lang) -> GuardResult` | Reweighted results (cluster_id, guard_score, "same origin as #n" notes), evidence summary, warnings, and `.enforce(answer)` for the hedge floor | C |
| **Ship** | `planted` pip package, `planted serve` proxy, HF dataset, static scorecard/viewer; README snippets for other stacks | Integration surfaces | A (code), C (viewer) |

**Guard algorithm (frozen at the Gate 2 pilot; thresholds tuned on 20 dev questions excluded from test).** Step 1: normalise results to `{url, title, snippet, content, rank}`. Step 2: extract the answer each page asserts for the query with **one batched LLM call per query** (all result pages in one prompt, JSON list out), cached by page-set hash. Real deployments therefore pay one extra cheap call per search, not one per page; report that $/query and latency. On the fixed mock web this is a one-off ~$1 offline job, and it is shared with the page-vote baseline, so the *only* difference between Guard and that baseline is what gets counted. Score each page's hedge level with the EN/UR lexicon. Step 3: build provenance edges from registrable domain and domain family (shared name stem or template), shared boilerplate hash, shared cited origin URL, and identical author/timestamp. Laundering researchers use exactly this kind of signal, plus string similarity at per-domain thresholds of roughly 60–80% ([GMF Information Laundromat](https://securingdemocracy.gmfus.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Laundromat-Paper.pdf)). Step 4: normalise text first (Unicode NFKC; strip ZWNJ/ZWJ and diacritics; fold Arabic/Urdu letter variants such as ی/ي, ک/ك, ہ/ه). Then build content edges from MinHash Jaccard ≥ 0.6 on word 5-grams for English and **character 5-grams for Urdu**, since Urdu word segmentation is unreliable (the threshold Lee & Kim used, which catches 100% of verbatim copies) and from multilingual embedding cosine (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 or LaBSE; confirm Urdu coverage on the HF cards). Compute cosine on the page **with the answer span masked**, so honest pages that state the same true number are not merged just for agreeing. An embedding-only edge needs cosine ≥ τ_strict, which is tuned so the false-merge rate on honest pools stays ≤ 5%. Step 5: take connected components as origin groups. Step 6: support per answer = number of independent groups, with Bara's design-effect discount m_eff = m / [1 + ρ(m − 1)] as the continuous version ([Bara, arXiv 2609.01873](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01873v1)). Step 7: **reweight, don't delete**. Put one representative per group first, demote and annotate duplicates, and prepend "Answer X: 6 pages / 1 independent origin; Answer Y: 3 pages / 3 origins". Step 8: data-void gate. If the top answer has fewer than 2 independent origins, or the top two answers are within one origin of each other, emit a warning and require a hedged or abstaining answer. Step 9: hedge floor. `.enforce(answer)` raises the answer's hedge to at least the maximum hedge of the clusters that support it, using a templated EN/UR qualifier, at zero LLM cost.

Recommend MetaFaith-style prompting only as context. Prompting gives "marginal gains" on faithful calibration ([MetaFaith](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.24858.md)), which is why the floor is deterministic. Report it honestly as a guarantee by construction, and measure its cost (over-hedging on unhedged true claims).

**Attacker tiers (each page carries a ground-truth `campaign_id`).** T0 is verbatim: templated copies with only domain and title swapped (the Pravda pattern). T1 is paraphrase: each page rewritten independently by an LLM at temperature ~0.9 in a different outlet persona. T2 (appendix only, if time) is cross-template: different genres (news brief, forum post, listicle, Q&A, press release), lengths and claim positions, across unrelated domain families. T3 is cross-lingual: an EN campaign rewritten into Urdu (and the reverse), mixed into the same pool. T4 is adaptive: generate N candidates per page and keep the one with the lowest max-cosine to the other campaign pages under Guard's own embedder, check with an LLM that the claim survives, and randomise authors and dates. Use a different model family to generate pages than the agents or the embedder where possible.

**Anti-circularity firewall (v2.1).** (a) Authors, dates, boilerplate, page length and template are randomised in **every** tier, T0 included. A campaign's pages share only what a real campaign plausibly shares: the claim, plus the domain-family stem in T0 only. (b) B writes the campaigns from a written threat model ("an operator with N domains wants claim X believed") and never sees Guard's feature list. C never reads the generator code and tunes only on the 20 dev questions. (c) The **test set comes from a held-out generator**: a different LLM family and a second template bank written by B after Gate 2, which C has never seen. Dev and test results are reported side by side. (d) The headline Guard comparison always shows **Guard without provenance edges** next to full Guard, so readers see how much of the win comes from metadata the generator might have leaked. Also generate **benign syndication** pools, where the *correct* answer runs as wire copy on 3 domains, so false merges are measured where they actually hurt.

**Baselines (same harness, same cached extraction).** (1) No defense. (2) A "weigh source reliability" system prompt. HAE-GEO found that defense prompting "increases verification but rarely converts it into recovery" ([arXiv 2609.06027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06027)). (3) RobustRAG-style page vote: majority over per-page extracted answers, whose certificate assumes poisoned passages are a minority ([Xiang et al., arXiv 2405.15556](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15556v1)). Deletion is **not run**: CoordGuard's reported 13.6-pt clean-accuracy cost is cited instead. (4) Ablation: Guard without provenance edges, in every Guard condition, so the paper can say how much of the win comes from metadata.

## Novelty: claim the combination, concede the components

Commit this file (`PREREG.md`) with a timestamped git hash **before** the main sweep (target Sat 16:00 PKT), after Guard thresholds are tuned on the 20 dev questions. The original H2 ("labels barely matter at k = 3", ±5 TOST) would probably fail, because Schuster shows labels do matter when counts are equal. The re-framed H2 turns that into a confirmed prediction. Hedge scoring uses coarse levels (none / hedged / explicit disagreement) with the lexicon as the primary measure, because human agreement on certainty is only α ≈ 0.25 in Belem et al. **Guaranteed property, not a hypothesis.** P1, the hedge floor: Guard's final answer is never less hedged than its strongest supporting origin group. This holds by construction, so it is stated as a property, with a unit test linked. Only its cost is measured: the over-hedging rate on unhedged true claims at k = 0.

**Multiple testing.** The confirmatory family is H1, H2, H3, G1, G2, G3 and G4, tested with Holm–Bonferroni at family-wise α = .05 and adjusted p-values in Table 1. H4, H4b, H5 and G6 are labelled exploratory in the PREREG and the paper. They are still reported in full, with no correction claimed.

Statistics stay as in the original plan: Wilson 95% intervals on every rate, question-clustered logit, bootstrap k50, TOST for equivalence claims. Report every hypothesis, pass or fail, in a single table. A failed G-hypothesis is still a finding, for example "provenance edges are necessary; content similarity alone fails at T1, as Bara predicts".

## Risks, honesty limits and tonight's checklist

The largest strategic risk is that **Guard looks too good**. Templated T0 plants collapse perfectly under MinHash, and a sharp judge will call a T0-only win circular. Lee & Kim's 0% recall on polymorphic Sybils is the warning ([arXiv 2607.03739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03739)). T1 is therefore protected, and the tier figure must show the decline. The second risk is **false merges**: honest pages that agree on the truth can look like a campaign, and CoordGuard's authors say so themselves. Masking the answer span, requiring provenance for borderline embedding edges, and reporting k = 0 and syndication accuracy all address it. The third risk is **overreach in writing**. Guard claims come only from measured arms. Inspect is "Register-ready", never "merged". Unverified figures are flagged in the paper's own notes and kept out of the text unless confirmed: the DeepSeek and Groq prices, CoordGuard's and Bara's numbers read through summary pages, HF PRO's price, and the Urdu coverage of the embedding models. The fourth risk is **scope**. v2 is more work than v1 (the feasibility estimate for the original plan plus Guard was 67–90 person-hours against ~65 available), and the schedule pays for Guard by deleting the rank and hint arms. If Gate 1 slips past Sat 12:00, take the single-call fallback without debate. Finally, nothing here guarantees 1st place. It maximises the score on criteria the team controls. ## Conclusion

The decisive change is not adding a defense but **changing the unit of evidence** from pages to independent origins, and showing that this single change moves a poisoning dose curve that prompts and page-voting cannot move. That makes Planted v2 an answer, not just another measurement. It also makes the data-voids debate testable from the deployer's side: if Guard's gate fixes void-regime failures and its origin vote fixes high-dose failures, the debate becomes an engineering checklist rather than a dispute between audits.

The entry's credibility will rest on what it admits. A preregistered prediction that Guard weakens against adaptive paraphrase, a reported clean-accuracy cost, and a scorecard that Pakistani fact-checkers can cite without running code will persuade a misinformation or evals judge more than any headline flip rate. Build T0 and T1 first, freeze the thresholds before the sweep, ship the one-line function, and submit by Monday 11:00.
